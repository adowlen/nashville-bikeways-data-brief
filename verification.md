## Join hygiene (Section 10.2)
- The fusion to the 2026-11-02 crash layer is a street-name match, not a proximity join and not a segment join: a crash name-matches when either side of its Street_Address, after house-number stripping and suffix normalization to the crash-address form, equals a normalized Bikeways OnStreetName. Match rates: 222,279 of 222,902 known-count crashes carry an address (99.7 percent match-eligible; 623 missing = 0.3 percent, below the about-5-percent flag threshold, and their pedalcycle mix cannot be checked by street because the missing field is the street itself - 1 of the 530 pedalcycle crashes is address-missing); 224 of 242 normalized bikeway street names matched at least one crash. The 18 unmatched names are listed in stats.json; most are compound entries recorded in the layer itself.
- Physical scale: network = 510 segments / 314.9 recorded miles across 244 raw street names, printed alongside every segment count.
- Built-in internal control group: the same name-match run over all reported crashes returns 100,306 name-matched crashes (45.1 percent of address-carrying crashes) - bikeway streets are largely arterials, so a high match share is the expected baseline for any subset; the pedalcycle figure (64.8 percent) is printed only next to that control. A second control: the crash layer's injury-or-fatal rate is 32.65 percent on name-matched crashes and 29.14 percent on non-matched crashes - a modest difference, not printed as a severity finding because the match is street-level, not segment-level.
- The HIN overlap uses the identical name normalization against the 2026-11-14 HIN snapshot's street names (175 normalized); it is a street-name comparison and is labeled as such in the brief, in stats.json, and in sources.md.

## Methods checklist (brief-writing standards, Section 3)
- Base rates and exposure: no traffic-volume or bicycle-volume field exists in either layer. Stated prominently in the brief's second paragraph and again in the fusion section; no exposure rate and no causal pattern is claimed.
- Direction of causality: considered - pedalcycle crashes name-matching to bikeway streets at a higher share than all crashes is consistent with several readings (more cycling where bikeways are recorded, arterial traffic mix, reporting patterns); the brief prints the comparison with its control and claims none of them.
- Category and definition changes over the window: BikewayType values are the layer's current labels; the layer records no type-change history, so era figures are described as the current inventory's recorded completion years, and the interpretation section carries that limit as the strongest alternative explanation. Status carries a two-spelling duplication (Complete / Completed), normalized and disclosed.
- Exact date range: bikeways snapshot pulled November 15, 2026; service data last edit November 12, 2026. YearCompleted range 2004-2026 on 509 dated segments. Crash layer window January 1, 2019 to October 31, 2026, as published November 2, 2026.
- Match rate for every join: printed above and in stats.json (99.7 percent address-present; 224 of 242 bikeway names matched; 172-of-175-style HIN name overlap is 70 bikeway streets in common, counted in stats.json hin_street_overlap).
- Small counts: every percentage is printed with its underlying count. The smallest group given a percentage is WOL (14 segments, 8.7 miles) - both numbers are printed wherever the type is characterized. Pedalcycle figures rest on 529 address-carrying crashes; counts are printed with every share.
- Official definitions: type definitions are the layer description's own (BL, BBL, SSR, WOL, PBL, PBBL expansions); "protected" in the brief always means the layer's PBL label.
